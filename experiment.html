The speed is not the story.
The cache is.

A 176B-class Qwen-based MoE (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, IQ4_XS quant, 84.9 GB GGUF) serves real agent traffic on two RTX 3060 12 GB cards through llama.cpp. The first request pays ~4m31s of cold prefill; every turn after that reuses 99%+ of the prompt and answers in 1.6–3.2 seconds. At 12–15 tok/s it is not fast. It is usable exactly where quality outruns speed: persistent agents and long-running batch review on hardware bought for gaming.

02 · Observations

what the numbers actually say
Prefill dominates

Four minutes of debt, then cheap

Cold prefill ate 271.4 s of a 272.1 s request. Steady-state generation is not the limiting UX factor; ingesting the agent prefix is.

271.4 s prefill / 0.7 s generation on t0

Cache is the product

Prefix cache turns 26.5k into 16

llama.cpp matched each new prompt against the slot by longest-common-prefix similarity and re-evaluated only the delta. Per-turn work collapsed from 26,499 tokens to 16–18, at 0.999 similarity.

170× cheaper warm turns · 272.1 s → 1.6 s

Speculation is not free

A draft model that barely drafts

ngram-mod accepted 2 of 64 tokens on the tool loop (3.125%) and that turn ran at half speed. Speculation needs an A/B baseline before it earns a flag.

3.125% draft acceptance · 6.90 tok/s on the same slot

Topology was hand-fit

A split that no symmetric rule gives you

-ts 1,1 loaded 15.9 GB of model onto one 12 GB card and OOM'd. 3,1 helped; 3.5,1 was the first split that started with the full 131k context. Early MoE layers sit on CPU, so the split is lopsided by design.

6.9 GB / 11.0 GB · per-card model allocation

80 GB-class, consumer VRAM

24 GB of VRAM, 85 GB of model

Roughly 34 GB of weights stay resident in 64 GB-class host RAM; the 39 GB sparse n-gram table lives in mmap page-cache instead of VRAM. That is the only reason this fits.

45.8 GB in-memory · 39.1 GB pageable table

Scope honesty

One machine, one operator

A field test, not a controlled cross-hardware benchmark. Build flags, CPU/RAM bandwidth, PCIe topology, page-cache state and prompt shape all move these numbers.

03 · Verdict

the case for big-sparse-on-small-GPU

For work that waits anyway, a large sparse model at ~15 tok/s beats a small model at higher speed. Quality is the deliverable; latency is not.

This is not an attempt to win chat latency. The workloads are batched review over files and project artifacts, where minutes of cold-start are acceptable, prompt caching makes every later step nearly free, and the answer being right matters more than the answer being quick. Consumer GPUs, one machine, no datacenter.

Quality-first batch & agent work Long-running jobs that tolerate cold start File & artifact review in-context Slot stays hot across a batch
